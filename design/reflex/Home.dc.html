WHAT IT AUTOMATES

A loop, not a funnel.

Each step is something a data scientist would otherwise do by hand.

01CollectEvery call goes to your LLM as today. Gargi logs the input and the typed answer. Or import months of history in minutes.
02TrainIt builds a small model (frozen sentence encoder + one head per field) on CPU, in minutes, and calibrates its confidence.
03ProveIt tests the model on data it never trained on. No pass, no swap.
04ShadowThe model predicts next to the LLM on live traffic. Nothing user-visible changes.
05SwapCalls where every field clears the confidence bar are served locally. Everything else, plus a permanent holdout slice, still goes to the LLM.
06WatchThe holdout slice keeps measuring. If agreement drops more than 5 points, the decision goes back to the LLM on the spot.

PROOF BEFORE PROMOTION

No pass, no swap.

A model goes live only if, for every field, on held-out data, it clears all three gates. The whole call must also meet the agreement bar, and it never trains on its own answers.

GATEDEFAULT
Agreement on the calls it would serve≥ 95%
Calibration error (its confidence means what it says)≤ 0.05
Coverage (confident on enough traffic to matter)≥ 30%
v1 · refused v2 · refused v3 · refused v4 · passed → shadow

In the demo, the first three training runs are refused. The model only goes live once it clears the gates.

THE CONTRACT

If anything in Gargi fails (no model, low confidence, a model that won’t load, a corrupt database, a bug), the call goes to your function, exactly once. Your exceptions propagate unchanged.

Tested by injecting faults at 19 points of the request path, in every lifecycle state.

WHERE WE’RE GOING ROADMAP · NOT SHIPPED

We’re building toward an autonomous distillation system.

Point it at your LLM traffic and it builds, chooses, tests, deploys and maintains the right small model for every repetitive decision, with no one tuning anything.

01Automatic model selectionTry several small model families; keep whichever passes the gates with the most coverage.
02Per-field servingServe the fields it’s sure of; send only the uncertain ones to the LLM.
03Self-healing driftRetrain on fresh LLM answers and re-promote once the new model passes the gates.
04One model, many tasksFine-tune a single compact model on the distilled data from all your decisions.
05An agent that runs itSchedules retrains, runs model searches, watches drift, proposes promotions.
WHEN NOT TO USE IT

Not every call should be swapped.

✕Open-ended generation or summarisation. It needs a fixed, typed output.
✕Answers that change week to week.
✕Streaming or multi-step tool-calling loops.
✕Decisions the LLM itself can’t answer consistently. Phase 0 found one; the gates refused to swap it, which is the point.
